Reading Reflections

Motherhood in Crisis

The story that I chose from the Motherhood In Crisis was chapter 4, “The Prisoner”. This story goes on about a mother, named Tetteh, who is pregnant, who moved away from her village and family, to live with her husband. Tetteh’s husband is abusive, attacking her as she’s pregnant. When she defends herself, she kills him, and is sent to prison, where she remains. Even when she finally gave birth, her child was moved to a facility, not being able to see her child as she remains in prison. From watching it through an interactive comic, it details and shows the things she has gone through, and how much pain she bears with the pregnancy as she is in prison. Even when they show the conditions of her new town, the jail cell, the prison, and even her husband. They put a lot more to Tetteh, as the conditions she has lived and gone through holds a lot to her character, as she remains brave throughout the tough things, with a child on the way. It holds more value because it showed the hardships that Tetteh faced, and hoping that everything goes well for her child in the end, and that she one day gets to see him again.

Motherhood In Crisis is entirely done within an interactive comic, specifically in chapter 4, it’s entirely done with illustrations and text. Each page in the chapter shows off the locations and the characters in different situations. From the first few pages, you are able to see Tetteh in prison, and the pain she is in, hoping that the baby is gonna be healthy, yet no one helps her. The illustration and text provides us with the conflict immediately, with her holding onto the jail cell, grabbing her stomach in pain. Although we could probably see it like a video or a photograph, the illustrations in the comic can show a lot more emotions and symbolism, as we get to highlight certain things within just simple colors, or the detail in the background or the characters. For example, on pages 10-11 on chapter 4, this only shows Tetteh’s husband, who she met in her village near Makeni, in 2013. In those two pages, it only shows her husband, behind a pinkish color, first seeming normal, smiling, with the text saying, “I was happy to marry him.” It then switches to a more red background, signifying it all gone wrong, and the husband angry, with the text saying, “But soon things changed.” Although you can do a very similar thing with photography, or even video, the illustration provides more detail on how bad Tetteh’s husband is, and it explains that character without needing to show anything about this person, or the things that they’ve done. Instead, it provides more of that character, and how they have affected Tetteh, and the actions that led to her killing him, and being sent to prison afterwards. This shows how the illustrations had advantages compared to photography.
