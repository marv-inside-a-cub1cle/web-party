Reading Reflections

On Photography - Susan Sontag

The opening chapter of On Photography by Susan Sontag shows how much an image is worth, and how much meaning it has, especially many years ago. The main part of the chapter mainly talks about how much a photo can mean, represent, and value. It goes on to say how the experiences and things that we do will always be similar to a photo, or even end in one (page. 24). It makes points of how later on, everyone relies on a camera to photograph these memories, and that these things make it feel like that it has to rely on it to know it exists. From this point that they are making, although I do agree that a photo doesn’t explain all of the events that are happening in the world, it still gives us a basis and understanding on how these things were around the time. As mentioned in the opening chapter, “Ultimately, having an experience becomes identical with taking a photograph of it, and participating in a public event comes more and more to be equivalent to looking at in photographed form.” (page. 24) From what they are saying, and what I interpret it as, is that photos that people take regarding these things that we do in life gives us an understanding of how everything was like, or currently like. It gives us a sense that these were what people thought about, these were what people were thinking about, and how life was like with it.

When Sontag explains how we are transitioning to only understanding images, and never the true experiences behind the images, and that we rely on it to paint that narrative for us, I disagree with that. I disagree because without these photos, we won’t be able to understand the feelings they are trying to send within these photos, or what they are trying to show with these images. We aren’t relying too much on images to give us what they want, we instead are able to express our own opinions, interpret what we may believe with what we see, and how we are able to reflect on that, and maybe show off and express ourselves from it. From capturing images and creating graphics, I am able to express myself with how I think, and what that image represents to me, while leaving others who view it to be able to express what they think it means, and also try to understand the feelings and things that I try to show off with just one image. I use photography and image making as a way to reflect my own vision and feelings on certain things, making people who see it connect to it. For example, I am heavily based on nostalgic feelings, and having an old quality to it, hopefully making people comfortable on seeing these photos. Images, such as graphics or photos, can hold meaning to everyone, showing that everyone can understand it, and that we are able to freely express ourselves.

Your Brain on ChatGPT - YT Video & Article from MIT Media Lab

From the video and article provided for this reflection, this shows how much artificial intelligence can make you lose a lot of cognitive activity, and further down the line connects to further issues down the line. It shows how dangerous AI could be to a person’s social and thinking skills, as people use it simply for conveniences and rely on it too much. However, as mentioned within the video, and how the “LLM-to-Brain” in the study says it “showed weaker neural connectivity and under-engagement of alpha and beta networks;” proves how less people will think for themselves and less to engage with anything. However, as mentioned in the video, AI can be used for good as a way to improve your thinking, and helpful by making things a little more useful, instead of just relying on it for everything. When watching the video and reading the article, I can agree that artificial intelligence has increasingly been a bigger part of people's lives now, as many companies, such as Google and X, push it out and advertise it as a convenient tool, as an everyday thing. The way they go on about AI makes it a lot more dangerous, as we see people get an attachment to these, and it starts being part of your life, as if they were always there. In the video, they mentioned numerous examples of how many people starting having close relationships with AI, and I believe it is really shocking and horrible of how it leads to a point where they can’t really focus or try to do things, and instead look for an easy way out, and soon create this attachment to something non-human like AI.

Going back to the video, one of the things they discuss is how younger people are more at risk to drop cognitive activity or social skills because of AI. The brain is a complex thing that we always must work on, as we are able to understand, and improve on situations, whether it is conversations with people, or doing tasks and assignments given to us. If a younger person were to have that activity, they will be able to freely think and do these tasks, and realize if they made any mistakes, and later improve down the line. However, as they use AI more and more, they soon lose that feeling, and soon feel like they aren’t really doing the thinking, and have someone else do it. With social skills, it’ll be worse as then they have grown an attachment to it, feeling more comfortable with talking about a lot of topics. They will not have to face the things that people face when being in a group, or something a little bit more harsher, as they will think that they will just agree to what they say, instead of realizing the topics in matter, how to process and think about it, and then being able to form your own thoughts and opinions. This shows how artificial intelligence can further provide risks if people in question don't know how to use the tools properly, and over rely on it too much.

Reading Reflections

Motherhood in Crisis - Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager, & Saidu Bah

The story that I chose from the Motherhood In Crisis was chapter 4, “The Prisoner”. This story goes on about a mother, named Tetteh, who is pregnant, who moved away from her village and family, to live with her husband. Tetteh’s husband is abusive, attacking her as she’s pregnant. When she defends herself, she kills him, and is sent to prison, where she remains. Even when she finally gave birth, her child was moved to a facility, not being able to see her child as she remains in prison. From watching it through an interactive comic, it details and shows the things she has gone through, and how much pain she bears with the pregnancy as she is in prison. Even when they show the conditions of her new town, the jail cell, the prison, and even her husband. They put a lot more to Tetteh, as the conditions she has lived and gone through holds a lot to her character, as she remains brave throughout the tough things, with a child on the way. It holds more value because it showed the hardships that Tetteh faced, and hoping that everything goes well for her child in the end, and that she one day gets to see him again.

Motherhood In Crisis is entirely done within an interactive comic, specifically in chapter 4, it’s entirely done with illustrations and text. Each page in the chapter shows off the locations and the characters in different situations. From the first few pages, you are able to see Tetteh in prison, and the pain she is in, hoping that the baby is gonna be healthy, yet no one helps her. The illustration and text provides us with the conflict immediately, with her holding onto the jail cell, grabbing her stomach in pain. Although we could probably see it like a video or a photograph, the illustrations in the comic can show a lot more emotions and symbolism, as we get to highlight certain things within just simple colors, or the detail in the background or the characters. For example, on pages 10-11 on chapter 4, this only shows Tetteh’s husband, who she met in her village near Makeni, in 2013. In those two pages, it only shows her husband, behind a pinkish color, first seeming normal, smiling, with the text saying, “I was happy to marry him.” It then switches to a more red background, signifying it all gone wrong, and the husband angry, with the text saying, “But soon things changed.” Although you can do a very similar thing with photography, or even video, the illustration provides more detail on how bad Tetteh’s husband is, and it explains that character without needing to show anything about this person, or the things that they’ve done. Instead, it provides more of that character, and how they have affected Tetteh, and the actions that led to her killing him, and being sent to prison afterwards. This shows how the illustrations had advantages compared to photography.
